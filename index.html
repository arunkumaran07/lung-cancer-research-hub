Introduction to lung cancer

Lung cancer is the most commonly diagnosed cancer in Canada, excluding non-melanoma skin cancers, and the leading cause of cancer death in both men and women 1. Worldwide it was the most frequently diagnosed cancer in 2022, with almost 2.5 million new cases, and the leading cause of cancer death, with an estimated 1.8 million deaths 2.

This section is a short clinical and scientific background for readers new to the field. Each statement cites its source, numbered at the end. It is a research reference, not medical advice.

Clinical background

Types of lung cancer

Lung cancers are grouped by the type of cell in which they start. The two main groups are non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and small cell lung cancer (SCLC) 4. Pathologists classify tumours using the 2021 WHO Classification of Thoracic Tumours, which works from morphology first, supported by immunohistochemistry and then molecular testing, and puts more weight on genetic testing than earlier editions 5.

Risk factors

Smoking tobacco is the main risk for lung cancer 12. In Canada, radon is the second leading cause of lung cancer after smoking and the leading cause in people who do not smoke; Health Canada estimates that it accounts for 16% of lung cancer deaths 13. Outdoor air pollution causes lung cancer, and second-hand smoke and some workplace exposures, including asbestos, increase the risk; a first-degree relative with lung cancer may slightly raise risk 12. Worldwide, an estimated 114,486 adenocarcinomas in men and 80,378 in women in 2022 were attributable to fine particulate air pollution 6.

Screening

Low-dose CT screening of people at high risk lowers deaths from lung cancer 14,15. In the US National Lung Screening Trial, three annual low-dose CT scans produced a 20% relative reduction in lung cancer mortality compared with chest radiography, although 96.4% of positive CT results were false positives 14. In the Dutch and Belgian NELSON trial, volume CT screening of men lowered lung cancer mortality at 10 years compared with no screening (rate ratio 0.76) 15. Ontario's organized program accepts referrals of people aged 55 to 74 who have smoked cigarettes daily for at least 20 years (not necessarily in a row) for a risk assessment; not everyone who meets these criteria is eligible for screening 16.

Diagnosis, testing and staging

Molecular testing of the tumour selects patients for targeted drugs, and when tissue is limited or hard to obtain, cell-free DNA from blood can be used to identify a targetable mutation 7. Stage is recorded with the TNM classification, which is revised periodically 17. The proposals for its ninth edition, built from 76,518 analysable patients, use new subcategories that split N2 lymph node disease into N2a and N2b and M1c metastatic disease into M1c1 and M1c2 17.

Selected trial results

Results below are summarized from each publication; all are randomized phase 3 trials except CodeBreaK 100. They are not treatment recommendations, and current Canadian guidance is listed under Guidelines.

  • Resectable NSCLC, stage IB to IIIA. Nivolumab added to chemotherapy before surgery lengthened median event-free survival from 20.8 to 31.6 months and raised the pathological complete response rate from 2.2% to 24.0% 18. After resection of EGFR-mutated stage IB to IIIA disease, osimertinib raised five-year overall survival from 78% to 88% 19. After resection of ALK-positive disease, alectinib kept 93.6% of patients alive and disease-free at two years, against 63.7% with chemotherapy 20.
  • Stage III NSCLC that cannot be removed. Durvalumab after chemoradiotherapy lengthened median progression-free survival from 5.6 to 16.8 months 21.
  • Advanced NSCLC with a driver alteration. Osimertinib as first treatment for EGFR-mutated disease gave a median progression-free survival of 18.9 months against 10.2 months with earlier EGFR inhibitors 22. In a single-arm phase 2 trial, sotorasib produced responses in 37.1% of evaluable previously treated patients with KRAS G12C-mutated advanced NSCLC 23.
  • Advanced NSCLC without a driver alteration. In tumours with PD-L1 on at least 50% of cells, pembrolizumab lengthened median progression-free survival from 6.0 to 10.3 months compared with chemotherapy 24. In non-squamous disease, adding pembrolizumab to chemotherapy raised 12-month overall survival from 49.4% to 69.2% 25.
  • Small cell lung cancer. In extensive-stage disease, atezolizumab added to carboplatin and etoposide lengthened median overall survival from 10.3 to 12.3 months 26. In limited-stage disease, durvalumab after chemoradiotherapy lengthened median overall survival from 33.4 to 55.9 months 27. After platinum chemotherapy, tarlatamab, a bispecific T-cell engager directed at DLL3, lengthened median overall survival from 8.3 to 13.6 months compared with chemotherapy 28.

Scientific background

The genomic landscape of NSCLC

The Cancer Genome Atlas profiled 230 resected lung adenocarcinomas and found a mean of 8.9 mutations per megabase and 18 significantly mutated genes. EGFR mutations were more frequent in women, and MET exon 14 skipping was found in 4% of tumours. MAPK and PI3K pathway activity measured at the protein level was explained by known mutations in only a fraction of cases 29. In 178 squamous cell carcinomas the same consortium found mutation of TP53 in nearly all specimens and a potential therapeutic target in most tumours 8.

Proteogenomics

The Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium (CPTAC) added deep proteomics, phosphoproteomics and acetylproteomics to genomic data. In 110 lung adenocarcinomas with 101 matched normal adjacent tissues, it identified therapeutic vulnerabilities linked to KRAS, EGFR and ALK events, reinforced the association of STK11 with immune-cold tumours, and found a smoking-related field effect in the normal tissue 30. In squamous cell carcinoma, CPTAC identified NSD3 as an alternative driver in FGFR1-amplified tumours, and its data argue for measuring RB1 protein and phosphorylation before concluding that CDK4/6 inhibition has failed 31. CPTAC proteomic data are public through the Proteomic Data Commons 32, listed under Data & portals.

Lung cancer in people who never smoked

Whole-genome sequencing of 232 lung cancers in never-smokers (the Sherlock-Lung study) found three subtypes defined by copy number changes, named piano, mezzo-forte and forte, and no strong tobacco smoking signatures, even in people exposed to second-hand smoke 33. Work on air pollution suggests that fine particulate matter promotes cancer from cells that already carry driver mutations. In mice, pollutants drew macrophages into the lung and triggered release of interleukin-1β, pushing EGFR-mutant cells into a progenitor-like state; in people, EGFR and KRAS driver mutations were found in 18% and 53% of histologically normal lung samples 34.

Small cell lung cancer biology

Loss of TP53 and RB1 is obligatory in SCLC, and inactivating mutations in NOTCH family genes occur in 25% of tumours 10. An expression analysis defined four subtypes, SCLC-A, -N and -P, named after ASCL1, NEUROD1 and POU2F3, and an inflamed subtype, SCLC-I. SCLC-I gained the most from adding immunotherapy to chemotherapy, and cisplatin shifted ASCL1-type patient-derived xenografts toward SCLC-I, which suggests subtype switching as a route to platinum resistance 35.

    How the data are gathered

    Once every 24 hours, starting at about 6:15 am Toronto time, a GitHub Action runs refresh.py, which queries the ClinicalTrials.gov API for … with status recruiting or not yet recruiting, and PubMed for the searches below. Trials that leave recruitment drop off the table at the next refresh. Papers stay in the feed for 120 days after PubMed adds them. The curated sections are edited by hand in curated.json.

      Limits

      ClinicalTrials.gov is the largest registry but not the only one; some Canadian and international studies are listed only in other registries. Automatic tags can miss or mislabel studies, especially basket trials that enrol several tumour types. Publication-type tags in PubMed are added during indexing, so the newest trial reports and guidelines can take several weeks to appear.

      Data from ClinicalTrials.gov (U.S. National Library of Medicine) and PubMed. Source code and change history are on GitHub.
